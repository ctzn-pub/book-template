## Headings mark sections

A level-two heading (`##`) starts a section; the table-of-contents drawer (the floating **Contents** pill, bottom-right) and the heading rhythm both key off these. Keep them short. Use a level-three heading (`###`) for a subsection inside a section.

Curabitur pretium tincidunt lacus. Nulla gravida orci a odio. Nullam varius, turpis et commodo pharetra, est eros bibendum elit, nec luctus magna felis sollicitudin mauris. Integer in mauris eu nibh euismod gravida.

<Callout variant="finding">
The `<Callout variant="finding">` block is for the one-sentence punchline of a section — the thing you want a skimming reader to take away. Use at most one per section, or it stops reading as emphasis.
</Callout>

### Callouts come in four variants

<Callout variant="caveat">
`variant="caveat"` (amber) is for a methodological warning — a survey mode change, a small subgroup, a definitional caveat. This is where you hedge a claim honestly.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="definition" title="Lorem ipsum">
`variant="definition"` defines a term you use throughout the chapter. Pass a `title` to label it; omit the title and it reads as a plain bordered note.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="aside">
`variant="aside"` has no card — just a quiet left rule and italic text. Use it for a conversational digression that would break the flow if left inline.
</Callout>

## Figures and charts

Wrap every chart, image, or table-as-figure in `<Figure>`. It supplies the caption, the numbering convention, and the responsive width. The caption should state a *finding*, not describe the chart ("Support fell 30 points" — not "A chart of support over time").

<Figure
  caption="Figure 1. A multi-series time trend rendered by <TimeseriesLineV1> with placeholder data. The series carry no real meaning — Group A rises, Group B falls, Group C holds flat — they exist to show the chart. Click a legend item to toggle a series; flip the “Show 95% CI” switch for error bars."
>
  <div className="not-prose">
    <TimeseriesLineV1
      data={byGroup}
      demographicGroups={['Group A', 'Group B', 'Group C']}
      demographic="Series"
      defaultVisibleGroups={['Group A', 'Group B']}
      density="medium"
    />
  </div>
</Figure>

A few things worth knowing about the chart wrapper. The `density` prop controls height: `"comfortable"` for a hero chart, `"medium"` for an inline figure like the one above, `"compact"` for small-multiples panels. The chart reads its colors from the active theme — political group names (Democrat/Republican, Liberal/Conservative) get the theme's party colors automatically; everything else takes a neutral slot.

<Figure width="page-outset"

      data={topline}
      demographicGroups={['Overall']}
      demographic="Overall"
      defaultVisibleGroups={['Overall']}
      density="comfortable"

  </div>
</Figure>

<SideNote label="Figure widths">
`<Figure>` takes a `width` prop: `body` (default, matches the text column), `body-outset` (a little wider), `page-outset` (wider still, for hero/comparison charts), and `screen-inset` (near-full-bleed, for punctuation moments). `<SideNote>` itself is this component — a small labelled note set off from the body.
</SideNote>

### Embedding an interactive

Not every figure is a chart you build in-house. To drop in a third-party
interactive — a Datawrapper chart, an Observable notebook, a Flourish story, a
YouTube clip — use `<Embed>` inside a `<Figure>`. It's responsive, lazy-loads
as the reader scrolls to it, and is sandboxed by default.

<Figure
  caption="Figure 4. <Embed> hosts any third-party iframe. This one is a placeholder video; swap src for your Datawrapper / Observable / Flourish embed URL. Watch/share URLs (YouTube, Vimeo) are normalized to embed form automatically."
>
  <Embed
    src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/aqz-KE-bpKQ"
    title="Placeholder embedded video — replace with your own interactive"
    ratio="16:9"
  />
</Figure>

Pass `ratio` (`16:9` default, or `4:3` / `1:1` / `3:2`) or an explicit
`height` for content that isn't a fixed aspect. For a fully-trusted source
that misbehaves under the sandbox, pass `sandbox={false}`.

## Tables

## Quotes and emphasis

A `<PullQuote>` lifts a sentence out of the flow at large size — the editorial pull quote.

<PullQuote>
The template gives you the layout vocabulary of a printed book; the prose is yours to write.
</PullQuote>

A `<Quote>` is for quoting a source, with attribution:

<Quote author="A. Writer" work="On Writing Well" year={1976}>
Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet — a quotation from someone else, set off from the body with its citation underneath.
</Quote>

You can also annotate a specific phrase inline: hover the <Annotation note="This is an <Annotation> — a marginal note attached to a specific word or phrase, revealed on hover or focus.">highlighted phrase</Annotation> in this sentence to see the note attached to it.
